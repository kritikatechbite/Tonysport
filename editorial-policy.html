Editorial Policy

Last updated: October 14, 2026

This Editorial Policy describes how Tony Sport Hub plans, writes, checks and corrects its content. It sets out the standards we hold ourselves to, and it gives readers a way to judge whether we are meeting them. We are an independent Canadian sports information website, and the trust of our readers matters more to us than speed.

Our Editorial Mission

Our mission is to help readers understand the sports they follow. We write guides, explainers and event information that are accurate, fair and written in clear Canadian English. We do not set out to break news or to compete with live reporting. Instead, we aim to be the reference that readers return to when they want to understand a rule, a competition format or a piece of sports vocabulary.

Editorial Independence

Our editorial decisions are made independently. We are not owned by, funded by or controlled by any sports team, league, governing body, broadcaster, athlete or sponsor. No outside organization reviews or approves our content before it is published, and no one can pay to have a topic covered or an opinion changed.

We believe independence matters because readers deserve to know that what they read is not shaped by commercial or political interest. If that ever changes, we will tell you clearly.

What We Publish

The website publishes several kinds of content:

  • Guides that explain sports, rules and competitions
  • Explainers that define terms and clarify how things work
  • Analysis pieces that discuss tactics or trends in general terms
  • Event information that presents schedules and details, clearly labelled when it is a demonstration

We do not publish betting tips, odds, gossip about private lives, or unverified rumours. We do not publish live scores or breaking news.

Source Verification

When we write about a rule, a competition or a fact, we seek out reliable sources. The best sources are primary ones, such as official rulebooks, league and governing body websites, tournament organizers and official statements. Reputable news organizations can be useful secondary sources. We are cautious about social media posts, forums and anonymous claims, and we do not publish them as fact without confirming them elsewhere.

Our verification steps

  • Identify the original source of each significant claim
  • Check dates, names and figures against that source
  • Confirm that rules are current for the relevant season or edition
  • Avoid relying on a single secondary source for an important fact
  • Leave out a statement we cannot confirm

Sports Information Standards

We apply the following standards to sports information:

  • Names, team names and competition titles are spelled as the organization writes them
  • Rules and formats are explained for the level of play being discussed, because rules differ between professional, amateur and international competitions
  • Dates and times include a time zone
  • Statistics are given with their source and period
  • Claims about records and "firsts" are checked before publication or left out

Because league structures and schedules change, we encourage readers to check the official website of any league or organizer before relying on our content.

Sample and Demonstration Content

Parts of this site present sample content to show how information is structured. Fixtures on the matches page use fictional teams and example dates. Sample articles on the insights page are original explainers. We label sample content, and we never present fictional results or fabricated events as real news.

If we later publish verified event information, it will be based on identified sources and updated with a date, and it will be kept separate from any demonstration content.

Fair Reporting

We aim to be fair to the people, teams and organizations we discuss. We describe events and ideas accurately and in context. We separate fact from opinion, and we label analysis as analysis. We avoid language that is unnecessarily inflammatory, stereotyping or demeaning. We treat men's, women's, para and youth sports with the same care, and we aim to reflect the diversity of Canadian sport.

When we write about individuals, we focus on public sporting performance and avoid intruding into private life.

Attribution and Use of Sources

We give credit where it is due. When we rely on a particular source for a significant claim, we say so, and we link to it where that is practical and useful. We express information in our own words and do not copy text from other publications. When we quote a short passage, we keep it brief and identify the source.

We respect copyright and the rights of others. We create our own illustrations and graphics, and we do not use official team or league logos. If you believe that we have used your work without permission, please tell us and we will look into it promptly.

Use of Tools and Automation

Content may be drafted or checked with the help of software tools, including writing aids. Whatever tools are used, a person is responsible for the content that is published, and the same verification standards apply. We do not publish unreviewed machine-generated content as fact.

Corrections Process

We make mistakes, and when we do, we correct them. The process is:

  • A correction request arrives from a reader or is found by our own review
  • We check the claim against reliable sources
  • If it is an error, we fix it promptly
  • For a significant change, we add a note to the page describing what was corrected and the date
  • Typographical and minor changes may be made without a note

We aim to respond to well-founded correction requests quickly. You can find how to contact us in the Contact section of the About page. See also the Corrections and Updates section.

Updates and Review

Sports information goes out of date. We review our guides from time to time, particularly after a rule change or the start of a new season, and we update them as needed. Where a page has been significantly revised, we aim to show when. Content that is no longer accurate and cannot be fixed may be removed.

Conflicts of Interest

Anyone who contributes to the website is expected to disclose any interest that might influence, or appear to influence, their work. This includes employment, financial relationships or close personal ties with a team, league, athlete or sponsor. We handle conflicts by changing who works on a piece, by disclosing the relationship to readers, or by not publishing the piece.

As of this writing, the website has no commercial relationship with any sports team, league or governing body.

Sponsored Content and Advertising Disclosure

The website does not currently publish sponsored content or display advertising. If that changes, we will follow these rules:

  • Sponsored content will be clearly labelled as sponsored, at the top of the page
  • Advertising will be clearly separated from editorial content
  • Sponsors will not control or approve editorial content
  • We will not accept sponsorship from betting or gambling operators, in line with the purpose of this website
  • Affiliate links, if ever used, will be disclosed

The Disclaimer and this policy will be updated before any such arrangement begins.

No Betting Content

This is not a betting website. We do not publish odds, tips, predictions for wagering purposes, promotions for betting operators or links to them. Editorial analysis discusses sport itself and is not intended as gambling guidance.

Reader Feedback

We welcome feedback about our content, including questions, suggestions and disagreements. Reader comments are not displayed on the site at present. Feedback sent to the site owner is read, and where it points to an error or a gap, we act on it. We may not be able to respond individually to every message.

Changes to This Policy

We will review this policy as the website grows and update it when our practices change. The date at the top shows the latest revision. Related documents include the Privacy Policy and Terms and Conditions.

How an Article Is Developed

Every article follows a simple path from idea to publication. First, we choose a topic that will help readers, such as a rule that confuses newcomers or a competition format that is often misunderstood. Next, we gather information from official rulebooks and other reliable sources. We then write a draft in clear language, with attention to Canadian spelling, terminology and context.

A second review looks at accuracy, clarity, tone and fairness, and checks every name, date and figure. Finally, we add a summary, a category and a publication date, and we publish. After publication, we revisit pages when rules change or when readers tell us about errors.

Language and Style Standards

We write in Canadian English. That means spellings such as colour, centre and defence, and terms that Canadian readers use, such as "hockey" for ice hockey, "Canadian football" for the Canadian game and "soccer" for association football. We use plain language and explain technical terms the first time they appear. We avoid hype, clichés and sensational headlines, and we write headlines that describe what the article contains.

Dates are written out in full, and times include a time zone. Measurements are given in the units used in the sport, with conversions where they help the reader.

Headlines, Summaries and Images

Headlines and summaries must reflect the content of the article. We do not use misleading or exaggerated headlines to attract clicks. Images are original illustrations. They are chosen to help the reader and not to suggest that the article is about a particular real event. We do not alter images to mislead, and we describe them in text for readers who cannot see them.

Categories and Labels

We use labels so that readers can tell what kind of content they are reading. Labels include the sport category, "Sample editorial" for illustrative pieces and "Example only" or "Demonstration data" for fictional events. We do not remove or hide these labels. If a piece is later replaced by verified information, the labels will be updated.

Handling Sensitive Subjects

Sports intersects with issues such as injuries, safety, discrimination and mental health. When we touch on these subjects, we do so carefully, with respect for the people involved and without speculation. We avoid graphic detail and we do not publish unverified claims about a person's health or private life. Where it is helpful, we direct readers to official or professional resources instead of offering advice ourselves.

Accountability

Editorial standards only mean something if someone is accountable for them. The site owner is responsible for the content published on Tony Sport Hub and for making sure that this policy is followed. We review our own work against these standards and update the standards when we find that they could be clearer or stricter.

If you believe that we have failed to follow this policy, we would like to hear about it. Please tell us which page you are referring to and which part of the policy you think was not followed. We will look into the matter, tell you what we found where we can, and make changes if needed. This is how we learn and how we keep your trust.

Contact Information

To send feedback or a correction request, please use the details on our About page.

Contact details have not yet been published on this website. The site owner should add a contact email address to the About page and to this section before launch. Until then, this notice does not provide a way to reach us directly.
