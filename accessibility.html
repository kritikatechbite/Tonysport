Accessibility Statement

Last updated: October 14, 2026

Tony Sport Hub wants everyone to be able to read and use its content, including people who use assistive technology, people with low vision, and people who prefer to navigate with a keyboard. This statement describes the steps we have taken, what we have not yet tested, and how you can tell us about problems. We want to be honest about where we are, so we do not claim formal certification.

Our Accessibility Commitment

We are committed to making the website usable by as many people as possible. We design with accessibility in mind from the start, rather than adding it at the end. Our goal is to follow widely recognized good practice, including the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) published by the World Wide Web Consortium, and we use them as a guide when we build and revise pages.

We have not had the website formally audited or certified by an independent body, and we do not claim conformance with any specific level of WCAG at this time. We will make any such claim only after proper testing.

Legal Context in Canada

Canadian federal and provincial laws address accessibility in different ways. Some provinces, including Ontario and Manitoba, have accessibility standards that apply to certain organizations. Whether and how those rules apply depends on the organization. This statement is informational and is not legal advice or a claim of compliance with any specific law.

Semantic HTML

The pages use standard HTML elements for their intended purpose. Each page has a clear main region, a header, navigation and a footer. Headings follow a logical order, so that people using screen readers can move through a page by heading. Lists are marked up as lists, and tables used for information have header cells. Buttons are used for actions, and links are used for navigation.

Pages declare their language as Canadian English, so that screen readers can choose a suitable voice and pronunciation.

Keyboard Navigation

The website is designed so that you can use it without a mouse. You can move between links and controls with the Tab key, and activate them with Enter or Space. A "skip to main content" link at the top of each page lets keyboard users bypass the header navigation. The mobile menu button can be opened and closed using the keyboard, and the Escape key closes an open menu.

Interactive elements show a visible focus outline, so you can see where you are on the page. The sport and category filters are real buttons that indicate whether they are selected.

The information notice page

On the information notice page, the two buttons are standard links and can be reached with the keyboard. Keyboard focus starts on the first button. Both lead to google.com, and the page states the destination.

Text Readability

We use readable typefaces at comfortable sizes, with generous line spacing and a limited line length for long reading, such as on policy pages. Text sizes use relative units so that they respond when you change the default font size or zoom the page in your browser. You can zoom the page and the layout should reflow without requiring horizontal scrolling on typical screens.

Colour and Contrast

We chose a palette of dark navy, white and red. Body text is dark on a light background, and light text on dark backgrounds is used for headings and footers. We aim for contrast ratios that meet commonly used guidelines, and we do not rely on colour alone to convey meaning. For example, the selected state of a filter button is indicated by both a change in colour and the button's pressed state, which is exposed to assistive technology.

We have not run an independent contrast audit of every combination. Some muted grey text and some text laid over images may not meet the recommended contrast in every case, and we are reviewing them.

Images and Text Alternatives

Informative images include descriptive alternative text. Purely decorative elements are hidden from assistive technology. The illustrations on the site are generated graphics and not photographs, and their alternative text describes what they show rather than naming real events or people. The full-screen background of the information notice page is described with a short label, and the page's essential content is real text, not part of an image.

Responsive Layouts and Zoom

The website is designed to work on phones, tablets, laptops and large desktop screens. Layouts adapt to the width of the screen, and buttons are sized so that they are comfortable to tap. The site can be used in portrait or landscape orientation.

Motion and Animation

Animations on this website are subtle, such as a gentle fade as the page loads or as sections scroll into view. If your device is set to reduce motion, the website respects that setting and turns off these animations. Nothing on the page flashes or auto-plays, and there is no moving content that you cannot pause.

Forms and Interactive Elements

The website has no forms to complete and no timeouts. The interactive parts are limited to the navigation menu, the filter buttons and expandable sections for frequently asked questions and article summaries. The expandable sections use native HTML disclosure elements, which are supported by assistive technology.

Known Limitations

We know that the website is not perfect. Our current known limitations include:

  • We have not yet tested with a full range of assistive technologies, such as multiple screen readers and voice-control software
  • Some muted text and text over images may have lower contrast than we would like
  • The fonts load from a third-party service, and if it is unavailable the site falls back to a default font, which may look different
  • External websites that we link to may not be accessible
  • Illustrations are stylized and do not include long descriptions of every detail

We are working to find and fix these issues.

Browser and Assistive Technology Compatibility

The website is designed to work in current versions of widely used browsers on desktop and mobile. We use modern but widely supported features, and the site remains readable if scripts are disabled, although filters and the mobile menu would not work. Zoom and text-resize tools in the browser are supported. If you notice problems with a particular browser or assistive technology, please let us know which one and which version.

How to Send Accessibility Feedback

We welcome your feedback. If you find a barrier on the website, or if you need information in a different format, please contact us and tell us:

  • The address of the page where you found the problem
  • What the problem is, and what you were trying to do
  • The browser, device and any assistive technology you use

We will try to respond promptly and to offer a reasonable alternative, such as providing the information in another way, while we work on a fix. Contact details are listed on the About page.

Accessibility Improvements

We plan to improve accessibility over time. Our next steps include:

  • Testing with several screen readers and with voice-control tools
  • Running a full colour contrast review
  • Reviewing page titles, headings and landmarks across every page
  • Considering hosting fonts locally
  • Seeking feedback from disabled readers
  • Updating this statement as we complete each step

Testing Approach

We check our pages in a few ways. We navigate each page using only the keyboard, we zoom to a large size to check that the layout reflows, we review the heading structure, and we look at the pages at a range of screen widths from small phones to large monitors. We also check that images have meaningful alternative text and that links make sense when read on their own.

These checks are valuable, but they are not a replacement for a full independent audit or for testing by disabled users. We say this clearly because accessibility is about real experience, and a list of checks cannot capture everything.

Navigation and Orientation

Every page uses the same header and footer, so that you can find your way around consistently. The current page is marked in the main navigation. Policy pages include a breadcrumb trail and a list of contents at the top, with links that jump to each section. Link text describes the destination, for example "Privacy Policy" rather than "click here".

On the sports page, a row of links near the top lets you jump straight to the sport you are interested in. On touch screens, that row can be scrolled sideways without affecting the rest of the page.

Tips for Using the Website with Assistive Technology

If you use a screen reader, you can move between landmarks and headings to find sections quickly. If you use a switch or voice control, the visible link and button labels match their accessible names, so you can say what you see. If you need larger text, increase your browser's default font size or zoom the page, and the layout will adjust.

If you find it easier to read in a high-contrast mode or with a custom style sheet, the website is built with standard HTML and CSS, which these tools can usually override.

Third-Party Content

The website loads fonts from a third-party service and links to external websites. We do not control the accessibility of those services or sites. If you find that a linked site is hard to use, please tell us, and we may be able to suggest an alternative source for the same information.

Our Commitment to Keep Improving

Accessibility is an ongoing responsibility and not a one-time project. Each time we add a page or a feature, we check it against the points in this statement. We will review the statement regularly and update it when we make changes or learn about new issues. We value the experience of our readers and would like to hear from you.

What Accessibility Means to Us

Accessibility is not only about meeting technical guidelines. It is about making sure that a person who wants to learn how offside works, or what a rouge is, can do so without unnecessary obstacles. People come to sports information with many different needs, including permanent disabilities, temporary injuries, situational limits such as bright sunlight or a noisy arena, and the ordinary effects of ageing.

We aim for designs that work for the widest range of people, without special modes that make some readers feel like an exception. Features such as clear headings, readable text, large touch targets and keyboard support help everyone, not only people with a recognized disability. We will keep these principles in view as the website grows.
