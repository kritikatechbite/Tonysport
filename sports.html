Introduction

A practical guide to the sports Canadians follow

Each section below describes the basics of the game, the Canadian context, major competitions and a few terms that help when you watch or read about the sport. League structures change, so always confirm details with the official organization.

Ice Hockey

Ice Hockey in Canada

Hockey is woven into Canadian life, from backyard rinks and community arenas to professional and international competition. Two teams of skaters try to put a puck into the opposing net, and games are fast, physical and often decided by small margins.

Hockey Canada oversees the amateur game nationally, while provincial and regional associations run minor hockey. Major junior leagues, U Sports teams and professional leagues give players a path to higher levels. Women's hockey has grown quickly, with a professional league now operating in Canada and the United States.

Basic rules and formats

  • Six players per side on the ice, including a goalie
  • Three periods of 20 minutes at most levels
  • Penalties send players to the penalty box
  • Offside and icing stop play

Major competitions

  • NHL
  • PWHL
  • CHL major junior leagues (OHL, QMJHL, WHL)
  • U Sports
  • IIHF World Championship and Olympic tournaments

How to follow

Start with your local team or a league's official website for schedules and standings. Radio and television partners carry games, and many leagues publish highlights and recaps.

Useful terminology

Face-off
The method of starting play, where two players battle for the puck
Power play
A man advantage after an opposing penalty
Hat trick
Three goals by one player in one game
Icing
Shooting the puck the length of the rink, which stops play

Basketball

Basketball in Canada

Basketball is fast, skilled and easy to start playing. Two teams try to score by shooting a ball through a hoop, and the sport rewards teamwork, shooting and quick decision-making.

The game's inventor, James Naismith, was born in Ontario. Today Canada has a professional presence, a domestic league, a national program and thriving school and club basketball in every province.

Basic rules and formats

  • Five players per side on the court
  • NBA games use four 12-minute quarters; FIBA uses four 10-minute quarters
  • Shot clock limits each possession
  • Fouls and travelling violations stop play

Major competitions

  • NBA
  • CEBL (Canadian Elite Basketball League)
  • U Sports basketball
  • FIBA World Cup and Olympic tournaments

How to follow

League websites publish schedules and standings, and many provincial associations list school and club events. Watching a few full games makes patterns such as the pick and roll much easier to spot.

Useful terminology

Pick and roll
A screen set for the ball handler, followed by a roll to the basket
Triple-double
Ten or more in three statistical categories in one game
Travelling
Taking too many steps without dribbling
Fast break
A quick attack before the defence sets up

Soccer

Soccer in Canada

Soccer is the most widely played sport on the planet. Two teams of eleven try to score with their feet, and a typical match is played over two halves.

Canada was one of three co-hosts of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and the domestic scene includes professional men's and women's leagues alongside thousands of community clubs. Canada Soccer is the national governing body.

Basic rules and formats

  • Eleven players per side, including a goalkeeper
  • Two halves of 45 minutes, plus stoppage time
  • Offside restricts attackers from waiting behind defenders
  • Cards signal cautions and ejections

Major competitions

  • Canadian Premier League
  • Major League Soccer (Canadian clubs)
  • Northern Super League
  • Canadian Championship
  • FIFA World Cup and Women's World Cup

How to follow

Official league websites list fixtures and tables. Many clubs publish match-day guides, and supporter groups are a friendly way to learn the game.

Useful terminology

Clean sheet
A match where a team does not concede
Set piece
A corner, free kick or throw-in
Stoppage time
Time added at the end of a half
Offside
A positional rule for attackers

Canadian Football

Canadian Football in Canada

Canadian football is a distinct sport with its own rules, field dimensions and traditions. It is faster in many ways than its American cousin, with more movement before the snap and an open passing game.

The Canadian Football League is the country's professional league, and the Grey Cup is its championship trophy. U Sports teams compete for the Vanier Cup, and community and high school programs feed the game's growth.

Basic rules and formats

  • Twelve players per side
  • Three downs to gain ten yards
  • Field is 110 yards long with 20-yard end zones
  • Single point (rouge) on certain missed or unreturned kicks

Major competitions

  • CFL
  • Grey Cup
  • U Sports football and the Vanier Cup
  • Junior football leagues

How to follow

Check the CFL's official website for the season schedule and standings, and look at provincial associations for amateur competition.

Useful terminology

Rouge
A single point on certain kicks
Grey Cup
The league championship
Three-down offence
The rule that shapes strategy
Motion
Receivers moving before the snap

Baseball

Baseball in Canada

Baseball is a game of pitching, hitting and fielding played across nine innings. It is slower-paced than many sports but full of strategy, and it has a long history in communities across the country.

Baseball Canada governs the amateur game. Professional baseball is available through a major-league club in Toronto, and summer leagues, including long-running Ontario circuits, provide local competition.

Basic rules and formats

  • Nine players on defence
  • Nine innings, each with a top and a bottom half
  • Three outs per half inning
  • A batter gets three strikes or four balls

Major competitions

  • MLB
  • Intercounty Baseball League
  • Baseball Canada national championships
  • World Baseball Classic

How to follow

Team and league websites publish schedules, and local clubs often welcome new spectators and volunteers. Scorebooks are a fun way to follow along.

Useful terminology

Bullpen
The area where relief pitchers warm up
ERA
Earned run average for a pitcher
RBI
Run batted in
Walk
Four balls awarded as a free base

Tennis

Tennis in Canada

Tennis is played one-on-one or in doubles, and it combines athleticism with careful tactics. Matches are made up of points, games and sets.

Tennis Canada runs the national program and the country's elite tournaments, including a major annual event held in Toronto and Montréal in alternating years for men and women. Canadian players have a strong profile on the international tour.

Basic rules and formats

  • Points are scored 15, 30, 40 and game
  • Deuce means 40–40; one player must win by two points
  • Sets are usually first to six games
  • A tiebreak can decide a set at six games all

Major competitions

  • Grand Slam tournaments
  • National Bank Open
  • Davis Cup
  • Billie Jean King Cup
  • ATP and WTA tours

How to follow

Tournament and tour websites publish daily order-of-play schedules. Court surface affects style of play, so notice whether a match is on hard, clay or grass.

Useful terminology

Ace
A serve the opponent cannot touch
Break point
A chance to win the opponent's service game
Deuce
40–40 in a game
Tiebreak
A special game to decide a set
